## ABO system
- Discovery: Landsteiner (1901), *Wiener klinische Wochenschrift* 14.
- Inheritance with three alleles at one locus: Bernstein (1924/25); Bernstein's estimator for allele frequencies.
- Molecular basis: the ABO gene (chromosome 9q34) encodes a glycosyltransferase; A and B transferases differ by a few
  amino acids; the common O allele (O01) carries a single-base deletion (261delG) causing a frameshift →
  non-functional enzyme. Yamamoto et al. (1990), *Nature* 345.
- Evolution: ABO polymorphism is shared with other primates — a long-lived **trans-species polymorphism**, likely
  maintained by balancing selection. Ségurel et al. (2012), *PNAS* 109.
- **Caution re. the brief:** "O first, then A and B by mutation" is a simplification; O alleles arose (repeatedly) as
  loss-of-function variants of A/B.

## Rh system
- RhD antigen encoded by *RHD* (chromosome 1p36); the common European Rh− haplotype is a whole-gene **deletion**.
  Wagner & Flegel (2000), *Blood* 95.
- Haemolytic disease of the fetus and newborn: Levine et al. (1941). Selection against heterozygous fetuses of Rh−
  mothers → unstable polymorphism in theory; anti-D prophylaxis (late 1960s) largely removed the effect.

## Selection evidence linking ABO to fitness (for Task 2 hypotheses)
- Malaria: group O associated with protection against severe *P. falciparum* malaria (reduced rosetting).
  Rowe et al. (2007), *PNAS* 104.
- Cholera: group O associated with more severe cholera (Glass et al. 1985, *Am J Epidemiol*) — verify.
- Norovirus: susceptibility linked to histo-blood group antigens / secretor status — verify (e.g. Hutson et al. 2002).
- COVID-19: ABO locus associated with severe disease; group A higher, O lower risk. Ellinghaus et al. (2020), *NEJM* 383.
